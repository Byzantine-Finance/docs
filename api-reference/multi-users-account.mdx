Multi-users account covers accounts with several signers, inviting users to an existing account, updating their roles, removing them, and allowing them to create their own passkeys.

## Which accounts can have multiple users

Both entity and individual accounts can have more than one signer (`root` user), each controlling the account with their own passkey:

- **Entity accounts** are multi-user by design: each root user and each beneficiary/representative is a separate person. Someone can be a signer without being a UBO, and a UBO without being a signer.
- **Individual accounts** can now also have several signers. Every signer is listed in the `rootUsers` field of the [create-individual-account](/api-reference/kyc) call, mirroring how entity accounts are created.

<Info>
  On any account, `userInfo` / `entityInfo` describes **who is being verified** (the customer), while `rootUsers` describes **who can sign**. For a standard individual account these are the same person; the sections below cover the cases where they differ.
</Info>

## Creating an account on behalf of a client

A wealth manager / CGP can create and manage an individual account for a client. This relies on the split between the verified customer and the signer:

- **`userInfo`** - the client. They are the KYC subject and appear as the account's customer from the moment it is created.
- **`rootUsers`** - the signer(s), for example the wealth manager, who control the account with their own passkeys.

```mermaid
flowchart LR
    A["1 - CGP creates the account: client in userInfo, CGP in rootUsers"] --> B["2 - Client is KYC-verified and is the account's customer from day one"]
    B --> C["3 - CGP invites the client to join as a signer"]
    C --> D["4 - Client accepts, creates a passkey, and can sign transactions"]
```

The client is the account's customer immediately, even before they sign anything. When they are ready to take part in signing, invite them with the standard [invitation flow](#accepting-an-invitation) below. Once they accept and create a passkey, they become a signer (or `root` user) alongside the CGP.

<Tip>
  To hand over full control, the client (once a `root` signer) can demote the CGP to a `view` user by [updating roles](#promoting-and-demoting-roles-of-a-member). This leaves the client in control while the CGP keeps only the access you agree on.
</Tip>

## High-level flows

### Invitation-to-account

```mermaid
flowchart LR
    A[1 - Account root user invites someone to join the account] --> B[2 - You notify the invited person on the account.member_invited webhook]
    B --> C[3 - Invited person accepts by creating a passkey within 7 days]
    C --> D[4 - Invited person is a member of the account]
```

### Updating roles

```mermaid
flowchart LR
    A["1 - Account root user updates the role of a member"] --> B["2a - view: member can only view account"]
    A --> C["2b - root: member can make deposit and withdraw transactions"]
```

What you sign, and what happens to the invited users, depends on the account:

| Account | Step 1 returns | Step 2 sends | Invited users |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Passkey (Turnkey) | `bodyToSign` | `signedBody` (the `bodyToSign`) and `webAuthnStamp`, stamped by a root user's passkey, with `invitedBy` | Receive a `pending` invitation with the `view` role, to accept within 7 days (see below). |
| Self-custodial | `typedData` and `issuedAt` | The same `accountId` and `newUsers`, the account wallet signature of `typedData` (`walletSignature`, `eth_signTypedData_v4`) and `issuedAt`, with `invitedBy` | Join the account right away with the `self_custodial` role. |

For a self-custodial account, `chain_id` selects the chain the signed intent is bound to (`1` for Ethereum, `8453` for Base, Ethereum by default) and must be the same in both steps.

<Warning>
  Byzantine does not send any invitation email. Each invitation emits the `account.member_invited` [webhook event](/api-reference/webhooks/accounts): notify the invited person from it, with the link to your own sign-up or acceptance page. `account.member_joined` tells you when they have joined.
</Warning>

### Following, resending and rejecting invitations

An invitation is `pending` for 7 days, until `expires_at`. It then ends in exactly one of these statuses:

| Status | When | Webhook event |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `accepted` | The invited person accepted it (step 3 above). | `account.member_joined` |
| `rejected` | You rejected it on behalf of the invited person. | `account.invitation_rejected` |
| `cancelled` | It was replaced by a resent invitation, or the user was removed from the account. | `account.invitation_cancelled` |
| `expired` | It was not accepted within 7 days. | `account.invitation_expired` |

Invitations can be listed per account or per email via the [get-invitations-by-account-id](/api-reference/account-management/list-invitations-by-account) and [get-invitations-by-email](/api-reference/account-management/list-invitations-by-email) endpoints. `expires_at` is returned for a `pending` or `expired` invitation.

- **Resend an invitation** - Invite again a user who rejected their invitation, let it expire, or has not answered yet. A new `pending` invitation with a new 7-day window is created on behalf of the same inviter, and emits `account.member_invited` again; a still pending one is cancelled. No passkey stamp is needed, so it is up to you to decide who may trigger it. It returns `409` when the user already joined, was removed from the account (invite them again with the flow above), has a more recent invitation, or when the original inviter is no longer a root user.

1. **Get remove users payload** - Request the payload that authorizes the removal, with the `userIds` to remove. Nothing is removed yet.

  horizontal

2. **Submit the signed payload** - Then call the below endpoint with the signed payload. On a passkey account, send `signedBody` and `webAuthnStamp`: the root user whose passkey stamped it is the remover. On a self-custodial account, send the same `accountId` and `userIds` (in the same order), the account wallet signature of `typedData` (`walletSignature`) and `issuedAt`, on the same `chain_id`.

<Card
  title="Remove users"
  icon="user-minus"
  horizontal
  href="/api-reference/account-management/step-2-remove-users-passkey-or-wallet"
></Card>

Some rules apply, whatever the account:

- A `root` user cannot be removed: demote them to `view` first.
- The account's KYC subject and the beneficiaries of an entity account (UBOs, directors) stay on the account with the `beneficiary` role: only their access is revoked (passkeys, OTP sessions). The response reports them with the `access_revoked` outcome, and the others with `removed`.
- A self-custodial account must keep at least one member.
- A pending invitation of a removed user is cancelled.

Each removed member emits `account.member_removed`, and each member who keeps the `beneficiary` role emits `account.member_role_updated`.

## Roles and permissions

Four different roles exist:

- `root`: The root user is the admin of the account and has full permissions.
- `view`: The view user can only view the account and cannot make any transactions.
- `beneficiary`: Beneficiary users are the associated persons of an entity account (UBO, representative, director or signer) at the moment of creation. By default, they cannot make any transactions. Being declared a `signer` in the KYB is a compliance declaration, not the `root` signing role.
- `self_custodial`: All accounts created with a self-custodial wallet have this role.

The roles and permissions are described in the table below:

| Role             | Can view | Can deposit & withdraw | Can manage users | Can add bank accounts |
| :--------------- | :------- | :--------------------- | :--------------- | :-------------------- |
| `root`           | ✅       | ✅                     | ✅               | ✅                    |
| `view`           | ✅       | ❌                     | ❌               | ❌                    |
| `beneficiary`    | ✅       | ❌                     | ❌               | ❌                    |
| `self_custodial` | ✅       | ✅                     | ✅ (with the account wallet) | ✅                    |
